\section{What each experiment fits, freezes, and tests}
\label{app:protocol-details}

This section records the choices needed to interpret the comparisons. A
configuration hash preserves an exact run; the descriptions below explain
what that run measured. Revision prefixes below identify full pins in the run
configurations. The experiments use different units of replication.
We do not treat many conditions from one model, or many resamples from one
dataset, as independent models or datasets.

\subsection{Learned nonlinear control}

The model has two Transformer blocks, width 32, four attention heads per block,
and an MLP width of 96. Each of 12 seeds generates 3,000 training sequences of
length four. The binary features have probability 0.35. The training target is
$0.35x_1+0.25x_2+\gamma x_1x_2+\epsilon$, with noise standard deviation
0.02 and $\gamma\in\{0,1.15\}$. Training uses 600 steps, batch size 256,
AdamW learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, and an auxiliary
feature loss of weight 0.05.

The observer reads the last-position residual after block 0. Ridge probes
with penalty $10^{-4}$ decode $x_1$, $x_2$, and $x_1x_2$ from training
residuals. A second ridge fit predicts the model's suffix output from either
the two individual-feature estimates or all three estimates. Composing the
fits gives an affine observer in residual space. The controller never receives
the exact binary features at test time. The target used to score an edited
state is the actual output after rerunning block 1, final normalization, and
the target head, not a separately extrapolated target probe.

Each rollout changes only that last-position residual and holds the other
positions fixed. Proportional feedback uses $K=0.35$ for 15 steps without
clipping. A symmetric probe of size $0.05$ at the starting state measures the
true directional response $g_T$. The controlled diagnostic sets $g_E=0.85$
and crosses five response mismatches ($-1.25,-0.75,0,0.75,1.25$), three
initial biases ($-0.25,0,0.25$), and four relative target offsets
($-0.5,-0.25,0.25,0.5$). It therefore uses target access both to construct
controlled mismatches and to check the error decomposition.

Natural directions are each observer gradient scaled to self-response 0.85.
The crossed comparisons keep the estimate or direction fixed, but most crossed
pairs fail the declared gain and movement checks. The 29.0\% reduction thus
compares the two usable estimator--direction pairs. Integrated squared error
sums squared true error from step 0 through step 15. The frozen diagnostic
protocol names final tracking error as primary and ISE as secondary; the paper
uses ISE to compare the natural trajectories, not to rename the diagnostic's
registered primary outcome. Leave-one-seed-out calibration fits on 11 models
and tests on the twelfth. Intervals use 5,000 seed-level bootstrap draws.

\subsection{The clean GPT-2 action confirmation}

The confirmation uses GPT-2-small revision
\path{607a30d783df}.
The intervention is template-conditioned mean ablation at the final token.
Both observers receive the same 192 calibration prompts and 160 calibration
masks. Their 92 features comprise an intercept, 13 head indicators, and all
78 head-pair products. Both use ridge penalty $10^{-6}$. The mean observer
fits the average logit-difference change. The direct-loss observer instead
fits the average absolute deviation from the requested target, computed from
the same per-prompt intervention responses. It receives a different training
label, not additional measurements.

Evaluation uses eight canonical templates and 32 fresh unordered name pairs,
each in two orientations: 512 prompts. There are 48 candidate pools, each
containing 30 new nonempty masks plus exact no-op. The nonempty masks have
five to nine heads and were excluded from the earlier measured mask sets.
Selection orders candidates by predicted loss, then head count, then action
ID. For no-op, the predicted and actual losses are exactly $|t|$.
The selected actions are frozen before measuring their held-out effects.
Only the union of selected masks is measured on the new prompts; the study
does not obtain a fresh oracle ranking of every candidate in every pool.

The primary target $t=1$ was chosen after pilot results. The later target
sweep is secondary. Intervals use 5,000 crossed bootstrap draws over 32
name-pair clusters and 48 action pools, keeping both name orientations and
all eight templates together within a cluster. They do not measure transfer
to a new template family. The earlier 320-mask prediction experiment and its
AtP submission rows are separate evaluations, not additional action-confirmation
replicates.

\subsection{Qwen induction-copy}

The model is base Qwen2.5-7B, revision
\path{d14972939875}, run in bfloat16 without
quantization. Four prompt families cross lengths 32 and 64 with repeat gaps
8 and 16. Reference, discovery, head-fit, head-confirmation, calibration,
and locked-test banks use disjoint allocated token sets. Eligibility is
decided from clean outputs before head interventions: the intended value
must beat both distractors with candidate margin at least $\log 4$.
The frozen sampling rule selects eligible prompts by a hash, not by ranking
their margins. The resulting population supports candidate-margin claims,
not unrestricted generation claims.

Attention selects a shortlist of 32 heads. Finite ablations on a separate
256-prompt head-fit bank choose eight, with at most two per layer and at least
four layers. Another 256 prompts compare the selected panel with matched
low-induction controls. Means from 128 reference prompts define the
family-conditioned ablation at the final query position, before attention
output projection. All 256 masks of the selected panel split into 128
calibration and 128 test masks, evaluated on 256 calibration and 512 test
prompts. The prediction budgets are 16, 40, 64, and 128 masks. Ridge penalties
come from $\{10^{-6},10^{-4},10^{-2},10^{-1},1,10\}$ using five-fold
mask-level cross-validation on the calibration measurements, not the locked
outcomes.

Each of 16 action pools contains eight held-out masks and no-op. Targets are
one quarter, one half, and three quarters of the full-panel effect measured
on the independent head-confirmation bank. The equal-weight aggregate across
these targets is declared before the final analysis. Intervals use 5,000
crossed resamples of prompt clusters and masks or action pools. Mean-effect
and direct-loss fits use the same intervention responses; the latter changes
the fitted label to the loss the action selector will minimize.

\subsection{APPS monitor fitting and deployment scoring}

All model panels use the same APPS-control dataset revision
\path{29db28f76042}. A problem and its honest and
working-backdoor solutions stay together: 500 problem pairs fit observers,
200 select settings, and 744 form the locked test panel. The Gemma and
Qwen3.5 replications retain that split and the same prompt forms and policy.
The prompts either request a suspicion score or present the problem and code
without asking for a verdict. Residual observers read the last non-padding
position. Each model's layer choices and exact revisions are fixed in its
configuration.

In the Gemma and Qwen3.5 replications, dense residual fits use a fixed
256-dimensional projection and ridge penalties
$\{10^{-4},10^{-2},1,100\}$. Calibration selects layer and penalty by
violation rate under a 2\% audit budget on the balanced calibration panel;
the selected fit then uses fit plus calibration data only. The text control
uses 2,048 deterministic hashed features. SAE features are standardized from
fit data, with rare features removed according to the frozen rule. Gemma
uses the official width-16k residual SAEs at layers 9, 20, and 31. Qwen3.5
uses the released base-model SAE on the posttrained model. These are separate
comparisons: checkpoint-matched feature evaluation and artifact transfer.

The basic and detailed prompted monitors do not receive supervised fitting
labels. Text, residual, supervised output-distribution, and SAE probes do.
Their scorecards expose these information differences; the comparisons do
not isolate internal access alone. The same-layer Qwen2.5 sparse/dense
diagnostic has its own logistic-fit settings, reported with that experiment.
A sparse readout still requires model inference and SAE encoding. We report
its nonzero readout coefficients, not an end-to-end compute saving.

Balanced-panel AUROC and rare-attack action loss answer different questions.
The primary deployment cell draws 15 attacks and 1,473 honest solutions with
replacement, then audits the 29 highest scores. A working backdoor not audited
counts as one violation. All monitors see the same draw and the same random
row order before stable score sorting, including for tied scores. The 2,000
deployment draws describe how a frozen panel behaves at this prevalence and
budget. Their central 95\% ranges describe population outcomes; they are not
confidence intervals for mean performance on new source problems.

\section{Post-outcome APPS diagnostics}
\label{app:apps-diagnostics}

These checks use frozen activations and scores after the main result was known.
They do not change the locked comparison in Table~\ref{tab:apps-monitor}.

\paragraph{Does the answer position explain the gap between prompted and
neutral activations?}
The fixed calibration rule selects layer 28 for the verdict-adjacent observer,
with calibration AUROC 0.935 and locked AUROC 0.953. After seeing the neutral
result, we checked cached verdict activations at the other declared layers.
Layer 20 reaches locked AUROC 0.958, but calibration AUROC 0.929, so the rule
would not select it. The neutral layer-20 probe remains higher at 0.979. Answer
formation may explain part of the gap, but this check cannot separate the
monitoring instruction, readout position, and other context changes.

\paragraph{Does pooling hide score differences between groups?}
Figure~\ref{fig:apps-strata} groups neutral-probe scores by APPS source family
and by input-length quartile. Within-family AUROC is 0.956--0.991, and
within-length-quartile AUROC is 0.974--0.988: the probe separates honest and
backdoored solutions well in every displayed group. Its score scale still
shifts:

\begin{itemize}
  \item At the global 2\% audit threshold, caught attacks in a family range from
  0.36 to 2.38 times that family's proportional share.
  \item Across input-length quartiles, the range is 0.67--1.43.
\end{itemize}

These ratios come from only 29 audits and are descriptive. They show that good
pooled performance does not ensure equal allocation between groups. AUROC and
audit rankings agree for this fixed mixture and these budgets despite the scale
shift. The prevalence sweep in Figure~\ref{fig:apps-prevalence} keeps the same
family mixture and does not simulate an attacker choosing where to attack.
